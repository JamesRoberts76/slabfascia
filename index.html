02 / Comparative Mechanics
TYPICAL TISSUE PATTERN

Dense & Rigid

Fascia has adapted to sustained compression by flattening into a load-bearing plate. No mechanical give. No elastic recoil. Tissue resists movement as a solid sheet.

BALANCED ALIGNMENT

Adaptive & Resilient

Tissue retains independent glide and mechanical bounce. Load distributes through micro-movement and elastic recoil rather than rigid resistance.

04 / Protocol Reference
View 7-Step Slab Release Reference Cards ▾
CARD 01
1. Surface Mapping
Identifying slab boundaries before attempting deep intervention. Mark the edges where density transitions to normal tissue.
CARD 02
2. Heat Penetration
Applying sustained deep heat to increase tissue pliability before manual work. Slab fascia will not release cold.
CARD 03
3. Edge Entry
Working at the perimeter of the slab rather than attacking the centre. The edges yield first and create a release pathway inward.
CARD 04
4. Cross-Fibre Disruption
Breaking linear adhesion patterns across the tissue plane. Slab fascia aligns in compression vectors; cross-fibre work interrupts the plate.
CARD 05
5. Bounce Restoration
Introducing rhythmic pressure to remind tissue of its elastic capacity. The slab has forgotten how to spring back.
CARD 06
6. Load Redistribution
Shifting daily mechanical patterns so the slab stops receiving the same compression vector. The plate forms for a reason.
CARD 07
7. Baseline Verification
Monitoring tissue response by touch, using spring-back as the primary indicator. Press and release. You might notice the tissue returning faster than before.
